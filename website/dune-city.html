How to Play Dune City

Dune City layers a Micropolis-style city simulation on top of Dune II. Build a Construction Yard, lay out roads, drop R/C/I zone tiles, and keep the lights on. The simulation does the rest.

1. Turn the city-sim mode on

On first launch Dune City offers a one-click Enable now prompt for the bundled city-sim mod. If you skip it, you can flip it on later under Options → Mods → Dune City.

2. Start a skirmish on a city-sized map

Pick Single Player → Custom Game and load the bundled map "2P - 192x192 - SimCity" (or any larger map with big rock areas). The city tools only work on rock.

3. Lay down the backbone

  • Construction Yard: your anchor — place it on rock first.
  • Wind Traps / Nuclear Plants: power everything else. Without surplus power, zones decay.
  • Refinery + Silos: spice income funds your build queue.
  • Roads: the build menu shows a Road tile when city-sim is on. Roads connect industrial supply to residential and commercial demand — without them, zones won't grow past Level 1.

4. Zone the city

The build menu also exposes three new tiles when city-sim mode is on:

  • 🏠 Residential (R) — population. Needs jobs nearby (Commercial / Industrial) and low pollution.
  • 🛍️ Commercial (C) — jobs and services. Grows when surrounded by people and supplied by industry along roads.
  • 🏭 Industrial (I) — production. Cluster it away from Residential — pollution drops land value.

Drop zones as 2×2 tiles. Each starts empty and develops over time based on land value, power, supply, and crime.

5. Manage the city budget

Population pays taxes annually. Police stations cost upkeep. Open the city budget window (HUD button) to set tax rate and police funding. Underfunded police means rising crime, which crashes land value and stalls growth.

6. Keep growing

  • Spread Police Stations across the city — they have a coverage radius, so clustering them is wasted.
  • Place Industrial in clusters at the city edge to keep pollution off Residential.
  • Watch the power meter — if surplus drops, growth halts everywhere.
  • The AI plays the same simulation. On large maps you can race the AI city's land value.

Combat still applies. Dune City is layered on top of Dune Legacy, so units, vehicles, ornithopters, sandworms and combat all still work. You can play a pure city-builder skirmish on a big rock map, or a hybrid where you defend the colony while it grows.

Troubleshooting: macOS "damaged" warning

The first time you download the macOS build, macOS may refuse to open it with the message "dunecity.app is damaged and can't be opened. You should move it to the Bin."

The app is not damaged. The GitHub-built bundle is ad-hoc signed (no paid Apple Developer ID yet), and any file downloaded by a browser is also tagged with a com.apple.quarantine attribute. macOS Gatekeeper refuses to run ad-hoc-signed apps that carry that flag.

One-line fix in Terminal:

xattr -cr /Applications/dunecity.app

After running that once, the app opens normally and keeps working across reboots. You only need to repeat it when you drag in a new version.

If you'd rather not touch Terminal:

  1. Right-click dunecity.app → Open
  2. macOS will refuse the first time — close the dialog
  3. Right-click again → Open → click Open Anyway

Linux and Windows builds don't have this issue — they run as downloaded.
