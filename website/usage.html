How players are counted

A player is a distinct in-game name among the recorded human players, or an anonymous installation ID once a game version sends one. Names that many people share identify nobody and are not counted: the game’s defaults (“Player” in the browser, “Mobile” on iPhone and iPad) and common computer account names such as “User”, “Admin” or a laptop brand. Most browser and iPhone/iPad games still use the default name, so those players are largely missing from these counts. One person using two names or devices counts more than once; the same name on two platforms counts once in the total. Player counts never show names.

Dates use UTC. First and current days/months may be partial. Dates before tracking began are not shown.

Game modes

DuneCity Campaign missions are counted as their own mode, not as Campaign. Network multiplayer is the reported game mode. Single-player can still have an internet connection or an AI sharing the player’s house.

What players like

Signs of liking: players come back on other days, play the same thing three or more times, or go on to the next campaign level. Signs of disliking: games quit before the end, especially within a minute. Each row is compared with the other rows of its table, so “liked” means better than the alternatives, not good in absolute terms.

      How these signals are measured

      Quit: a game that reported an early exit, as a share of games with an end report; a game without an end report is neither quit nor finished. Quit within 1 minute uses games with an observed start and end. Players, played 3+ times and came back on another day (UTC) count identified players only, as described under Players. For campaign levels and DuneCity missions, replaying is often retrying a hard mission, so they use “went on to next” instead: players of a level who also started the next level in this period. Typical finished game is the median length of finished games. A verdict needs at least 20 games and 5 identified players. Liked: players come back or play again at least 20% more often than across the table, go on 20% more often, or quit 20% less. Disliked: quit 25% more, quit within a minute 50% more, or come back or go on 40% less. Rows with signs both ways are mixed.

      How often players win

      A try is a game that was won, lost, or quit after at least five minutes of game time. The win rate is wins per try. Games left in the first five minutes are listed apart and are not tries.

      How wins are counted

      Won: the game finished and a human player won. Lost: it finished and the human was defeated. Players rarely play on to a defeat, so quitting after five minutes or more counts as a try that was not won. Game time is the time the game itself reports, which excludes pauses. A game continued from a save is recorded as a new game, so a mission played over two sittings appears as one quit and one win; win rates per game are therefore lower than the share of missions players eventually win. “Players who won” counts identified players with at least one try, and those of them who won at least once. Difficulty is the QuantBot difficulty of the opponents; games against several different difficulties are “Mixed”, and games without a QuantBot opponent are not listed there. Only games from version 1.0.865 onward are counted: earlier versions usually sent no end report when a campaign mission was won, so their wins are missing.
